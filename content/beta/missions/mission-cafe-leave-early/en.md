# Leaving in Time

## target
On a terrace visit, stand up and leave while {name} is still lying calm, before any overload signal.

## success
You leave with {name} relaxed on a slack leash.

## problems
### late
- label: They were already overloaded
- reassure: This happens a lot. Judging the time is hard at first.
- cause: The visit ran longer than {name} could manage.
- c1: Decide your leaving time before you sit down.
- c2: Shorten the visit next time, even if {name} seems fine.
- easier: A 5-minute visit and then leave, 1 time.
- alt: A clock next to a coffee cup
- caption: Decide your time when you sit down

### notice
- label: I did not see the signals
- reassure: The signals are subtle. You learn them with practice.
- cause: You were on your phone or chatting and not watching {name}.
- c1: Glance at {name} between sips.
- c2: The signals are standing up, panting, whining, staring at food or refusing a treat.
- easier: Watch {name} on the mat at home for 2 minutes, 2 times.
- alt: A person watches their Dog lying on the mat
- caption: Glance at {name} between sips

### hurry
- label: I left in a hurry
- reassure: Leaving fast is normal if the visit goes wrong. It is fine.
- cause: You stayed until it became urgent.
- c1: Ask for the bill early, before the time you set.
- c2: If you must leave now, pay at the counter with {name} beside you, without pulling on the leash.
- easier: Ask for the bill while {name} is still calm, 1 time.
- alt: A person gathers the mat calmly
- caption: Pack up calmly

## general
- reassure: Leaving when everything is going well is hard.
- cause: The common mistake is staying "a little longer" and going past your time.
- c1: Set your leaving time before you sit down.
- c2: Review the lesson on leaving in time.
- easier: Leave after 5 minutes with {name} calm, 1 time.
- alt: A person stands up from the table with their Dog
- caption: Leaving in time
