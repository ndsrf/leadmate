# The Harness at Home

## target
Put the harness on your Dog only when standing still, without physically restraining.

## success
Remains standing still or seated while you slip the harness over its head.

## problems
### excited
- label: Becomes overexcited at the sight of the harness
- reassure: Completely normal: the harness cues the walk, sparking arousal in any Dog.
- cause: The harness usually appears immediately before departing, turning its appearance into an arousal trigger.
- c1: Bring out the harness multiple times a day without heading out. Leave it in view and put it away once calm.
- c2: Move the harness forward only slightly. If the Dog jumps, withdraw it and wait in silence.
- easier: Stand still while you hold the harness one meter away, 3 times.
- alt: A Handler holds the harness at a distance while the Dog settles
- caption: Keep the harness back until calm returns

### runs
- label: Darts away
- reassure: Common for many Dogs, especially if the harness chafes or previously caused a scare.
- cause: May associate the harness with physical discomfort or being chased down to gear up.
- c1: Do not chase. Sit on the floor holding the harness and reward voluntary approaches.
- c2: Check the fit: two fingers should slip comfortably between the harness straps and the body.
- easier: Approach the harness voluntarily to sniff it, 3 times.
- alt: A Handler sits on the floor with the harness and a treat in hand
- caption: Allow voluntary approach

### bites
- label: Bites the harness
- reassure: Mouthing the harness channels play drive or nervous displacement energy. It can be redirected.
- cause: High arousal combined with shifting straps makes the harness resemble a tug toy.
- c1: Move the harness slowly. If grabbed, hold still and wait for release.
- c2: Offer a licked treat from your other hand while sliding the harness over the head.
- easier: Slip the harness over the head while licking a treat, 2 times.
- alt: The Dog licks a treat while the Handler slides the harness on
- caption: Lick treat for the mouth

## general
- reassure: A clumsy first attempt is a normal part of the learning curve. Keep practicing.
- cause: Advancing criteria too quickly before the Dog understands the exercise is typical.
- c1: Break the procedure into smaller micro-steps and reward each one.
- c2: Practice during rested, low-energy moments, not immediately prior to a walk.
- easier: Remain still with the harness in plain sight, 3 times.
- alt: A Handler practices with the harness during a calm moment
- caption: Small steps
