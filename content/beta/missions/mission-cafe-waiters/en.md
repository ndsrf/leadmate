# Waiters and People Passing By

## target
At a real terrace, let two waiters or passers-by go by with a short slack leash, {name} on the wall side and your body toward the walkway.

## success
{name} stays lying down, or looks back at you, as people pass.

## problems
### tight
- label: The leash went tight
- reassure: Gathering the leash without tension is hard at first. That is normal.
- cause: The leash was short but taut, and {name} feels it.
- c1: Gather with both hands and keep a soft curve.
- c2: If you cannot, leave more room or change tables.
- easier: Practise gathering a slack leash with your Dog beside you, 2 times.
- alt: A person gathers the leash keeping a soft curve
- caption: Short, but slack

### stands
- label: Stood up when someone passed
- reassure: Many dogs find this hard at first. It is not your fault or theirs.
- cause: There was little distance to the walkway or it was a very busy passage.
- c1: Move {name} closer to the wall and put your body between them and the passage.
- c2: If it stays hard, change tables or shorten the visit.
- easier: Sit for 5 minutes at a quiet corner table, 1 time.
- alt: A quiet corner table on a terrace
- caption: A quiet corner

### nospot
- label: There was no wall table
- reassure: There is not always a good spot. You can pick another day or venue.
- cause: The terrace was full or had no corners.
- c1: Choose a quieter hour, or a terrace with a corner.
- c2: Ask the venue whether you can sit where you prefer.
- easier: Visit a quiet terrace at an off-peak hour, 1 time.
- alt: An almost empty terrace in mid-afternoon
- caption: A quiet hour

## general
- reassure: Waiters and passers-by are a real challenge.
- cause: The common mistake is sitting at a table too close to the passage.
- c1: Start with a spot where few people pass.
- c2: Review the lesson on waiters and passers-by.
- easier: Sit for 5 minutes in a quiet corner, 1 time.
- alt: A quiet corner of a terrace
- caption: A quiet corner
