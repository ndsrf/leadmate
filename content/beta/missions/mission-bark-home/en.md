# Rewarding Silence

## target
When {name} barks, remove the Distractor or wait without speaking, and as soon as there is a pause in silence, use your Marker and deliver a treat. Do this five times in one day.

## success
There are longer pauses between barks and {name} settles faster than on day one.

## problems
### nonstop
- label: Barks non-stop
- reassure: It can take time at first. It is not failing: the Dog is learning that barking no longer pays off.
- cause: The Distractor is likely still present: the window, the hallway, or outside noise.
- c1: Remove the Distractor: close the curtain or move with the Dog to a quieter room.
- c2: Wait there without speaking and reward the very first pause, even if it lasts only one second.
- easier: Pause in another room, away from the Distractor, 2 times.
- alt: A closed curtain with the Dog lying down calmly in front of it
- caption: Removing the Distractor

### shout
- label: I end up yelling
- reassure: Almost everyone experiences this: barking is exhausting and waiting in silence is hard.
- cause: To {name}, your shouting sounds like more barking, driving arousal higher.
- c1: Decide in advance how long you will wait and breathe slowly; use headphones if helpful.
- c2: Have the treat ready for the pause, not as a bribe to stop barking.
- easier: Wait one minute in silence before doing anything, 2 times.
- alt: The Handler waits in silence with a treat concealed in hand
- caption: Wait and reward the pause

### trigger
- label: I cannot remove the Distractor
- reassure: You cannot always remove the Distractor completely; you can reduce its impact.
- cause: Sometimes the Distractor is the entire street or neighbors, outside your control.
- c1: Move the Dog's resting area to a room without windows facing the street.
- c2: Play soft music or white noise to buffer hallway sounds.
- easier: Let the Dog rest for a while each day in the quietest room.
- alt: The Dog rests in a windowless room with soft music playing
- caption: A space free of Distractors

## general
- reassure: Waiting for silence is demanding. Every time you succeed, {name} learns.
- cause: Starting with the most challenging scenario is the most common trap.
- c1: Practice first with something mild, like a faint noise, before trying with a visitor.
- c2: Review the "Barking at Home" lesson.
- easier: Reward a single moment of silence with a low-level Distractor.
- alt: The Dog, lying calmly, receives a treat from the Handler
- caption: Start with what is easy
