# Calm First

## target
When barking constantly or dashing through the house occurs, wait motionless and silent until calm returns. Ask for a Cue only once calm is restored, and reward that calm.

## success
Settles faster than on day one, then looks at you and listens.

## problems
### neverstops
- label: Never calms down
- reassure: It may take considerable time at first. It is not failing: the Dog is learning that barking no longer yields results.
- cause: The Distractor is likely still present: the window, visitors, or noise.
- c1: Remove the Distractor: close the curtain, or move with the Dog to a calmer room.
- c2: Wait there without speaking. Count the seconds and write them down: you will see them decrease.
- easier: Settle in another room away from the Distractor, 2 times.
- alt: A closed curtain with the Dog lying down calmly before it
- caption: Remove the Distractor

### escalates
- label: Arousal climbs even higher
- reassure: It is normal for arousal to spike briefly before coming down.
- cause: Something in your posture might be adding energy: quick movements, talking, or direct eye contact.
- c1: Stand still with arms resting at your sides and your gaze averted.
- c2: Breathe slowly and silently: your stillness helps lower arousal.
- easier: Arousal drops slightly while you wait motionless, 2 times.
- alt: The Handler stands still with arms down, looking away
- caption: Add no energy

### givein
- label: I end up speaking or giving something
- reassure: Almost everyone slips up: barking is draining and remaining inactive is difficult.
- cause: A word or a treat ultimately reinforced the barking.
- c1: Decide beforehand how long you will wait, and wear headphones if it helps.
- c2: Have the treat ready for when calm is reached, never to buy silence.
- easier: Wait one minute in silence before taking any action, 2 times.
- alt: The Handler waits with a treat tucked away while the Dog settles
- caption: Treats are for calm

## general
- reassure: Waiting out arousal takes discipline. Every time you hold the boundary, your Dog learns.
- cause: Starting with the most challenging distraction is the typical error.
- c1: Practice first with mild Distractors, such as low background noise, before testing with visitors.
- c2: Review the "Calm First" lesson.
- easier: Wait for calm once with a mild Distractor.
- alt: The Dog, resting calmly, receives a treat from the Handler
- caption: Start with what is easy
