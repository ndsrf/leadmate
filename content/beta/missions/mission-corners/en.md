# Neighborhood Corners

## target
Slow down before every corner on your block and turn it with your Dog at your side.

## success
You navigate corners with Slack in the leash.

## problems
### ahead
- label: Always surges ahead
- reassure: Forging ahead is natural for most Dogs. Exploring is what they love.
- cause: You likely slowed down too late, right at the corner.
- c1: Begin slowing three or four paces before the corner.
- c2: Deliver rewards at your side, level with your hip.
- easier: Slow down and reward before 2 corners.
- alt: A Handler slows down a few steps before the corner
- caption: Slow down early

### surprise
- label: Got startled by something
- reassure: A surprise at a corner is precisely what we want to avoid. You handled it well by moving away.
- cause: Something appeared suddenly and your Dog had no safety margin.
- c1: Take a wide arc: turn along the widest part of the sidewalk.
- c2: After a scare, pause and allow sniffing.
- easier: Navigate 2 quiet corners along the wide arc.
- alt: A Handler turns wide along the outside of the sidewalk
- caption: Take a wide arc

### forget
- label: Forgot to slow down
- reassure: Forgetting is completely natural: it is a brand-new reflex for you too.
- cause: Corners sneak up on you unless you actively scan for them.
- c1: Decide which corners you will practice before stepping out.
- c2: Use a personal prompt, like touching the leash, as soon as you spot a corner.
- easier: Practice at a single designated corner, 2 times.
- alt: A simple map of a street block highlighting corners
- caption: Choose corners in advance

## general
- reassure: Every corner presents unique sightlines. Consistency is what counts.
- cause: Attempting every single corner on the route at once is the common pitfall.
- c1: Start with corners that offer open visibility.
- c2: Review the blind corner theory lesson.
- easier: Turn one corner with clear sightlines, 2 times.
- alt: A corner with clear, open visibility
- caption: Easy corners first
