# The Right Cue

## target
On a walk down a busy street, use "Leave It" for ground food and "Let's Go" for fast movers.

## success
You choose the right Cue without hesitation at least twice.

## problems
### mixup
- label: I mixed them up
- reassure: Mixing them up early on happens to everyone.
- cause: Managing two new Cues simultaneously is a heavy mental load.
- c1: Before heading out, repeat the rule: food means "Leave It"; speed means "Let's Go".
- c2: When in doubt, perform a Poste: it always works.
- easier: Deliver a single Cue accurately, 2 times.
- alt: A prompt card showing two Cues: food and a scooter
- caption: One Cue per scenario

### toomuch
- label: There was too much going on
- reassure: A crowded sidewalk drains anyone, Handler or Dog.
- cause: The street presented more Distractors than you could process.
- c1: Choose a quieter time of day.
- c2: Focus only on fast movers; ignore the rest.
- easier: A 5-minute walk down a moderately quiet street.
- alt: A city sidewalk during a quiet time of day
- caption: A quiet hour

### nothing
- label: Did not respond to either Cue
- reassure: If the Dog does not respond, arousal is too high to process information. It is not defiance.
- cause: Arousal crossed the threshold where focus is possible.
- c1: Increase distance before delivering any Cue.
- c2: Allow decompression sniffing to lower arousal.
- easier: One successful Cue response on a quiet street.
- alt: A Dog sniffs peacefully away from street traffic
- caption: Lower arousal

## general
- reassure: Choosing the appropriate Cue under pressure is an advanced skill. You are on the right track.
- cause: Expecting perfection on the very first outing is common.
- c1: Practice each Cue independently on separate days.
- c2: Review the lesson on choosing Cues.
- easier: Deliver one Cue with precise timing, 1 time.
- alt: Practicing a single Cue on an open sidewalk
- caption: One Cue at a time
