# At Home · Do I Open the Door?

## primer
### card 1
Remember: if it is only protesting, wait; if there is panic or the time limit is reached, open the door. If the Dog freezes up, shorten the step. If calm, use the Marker and reward.
- caption: A small, familiar space with the door open

## checkpoint
### tq1
{name} whines behind the door but is breathing normally. What do you do?
- [x] Wait without speaking or opening the door
- [ ] Open immediately so it does not suffer
- [ ] Scold it to make it quiet
- why: If it is only protesting, opening teaches that whining opens the door; wait, with a time limit.

### tq2
{name} drools, scratches at the door, and refuses treats. What do you do?
- [x] Open the door and step back to an easier step
- [ ] Wait for it to tire out
- [ ] Close the door tighter
- why: That is panic, not frustration: open immediately, stop training if it repeats, and consult a professional.

### tq3
More than 10 minutes of continuous crying have passed with an adult Dog. What do you do?
- [x] Open the door and shorten the step next time
- [ ] Keep waiting until it gives up
- [ ] Yell from outside
- why: The maximum time is 10 minutes (2 minutes for puppies under 4 months): past that limit, always open the door.

## exercises
- timing: At home: with the door open, leave a treat in its space and see if it enters on its own.
- tension: At home: do a one-second micro-closure with the door and open it with your release Cue.
