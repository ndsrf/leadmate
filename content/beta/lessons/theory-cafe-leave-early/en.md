# Leaving in Time

## heading
You wait until {name} cannot cope any more

## hook
You are enjoying your coffee and {name} has been on the mat for a good while. A few minutes ago they stood up and lay down again, and now they pant a little and stare at the plate on the next table. You think they can last a bit longer, but by the time they start whining and pulling on the leash, it is too late to leave calmly.

## why
A dog gets tired of staying still among so much going on, just like a person in a long meeting. The signals arrive before the meltdown: getting up off the mat, panting when it is not hot, whining, staring at food or refusing a treat. Each signal is a warning that it is getting harder. If you leave then, the visit ends well and {name} remembers the terrace as a calm place. If you wait until they are overwhelmed, they learn the opposite. Leaving early is not failing: it is part of the training. And tomorrow you can come back, with a slightly shorter visit.

## steps
1. Watch {name} between sips. The signals are getting off the mat, panting, whining, staring at food or refusing a treat.
2. Decide your leaving time before you sit down, for example at 10 minutes, and do not wait until {name} is overwhelmed.
3. If you see a signal and {name} is still calm, say "good", give a treat and pack up without hurrying.
4. Ask for the bill, gather the mat and stand up calmly. If you must leave now, pay inside or later, without pulling on the leash.

## example
### {name} and the second coffee
You are on the terrace and {name} has lain calmly for ten minutes. Your coffee arrives and, a few minutes later, {name} stands up and lies down again. It is a first signal. You remember what you decided on arrival, say "good", drop a treat and pick up the mat. {name} gets up with you, relaxed, and you leave unhurried. On the way home they sniff a tree. Tomorrow you will come back for another ten minutes, and it will be easier.

## compare
### do
Leave as soon as you see the first signal while {name} is still calm. Reward, pack up calmly and go.
### dont
Wait for "a little longer" until {name} whines, pants and pulls on the leash. You leave in a hurry and the memory of the terrace is a bad one.

## tip
Try it today: before you sit down, decide what time you leave and note it. When it comes, go even if {name} is calm.

## takeaways
- The signals arrive before the meltdown: watch for them.
- Leave while {name} is still calm.
- A short good visit beats a long bad one.
- Leaving in time is training too.
