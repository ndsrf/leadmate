# Handling and Vet Visits

## heading
Touching paws, ears, and mouth calmly

## hook
When it is time to trim nails, clean ears after a walk, or receive a vaccine, {name} squirms, yanks paws away with force, and desperately tries to break free. Restraining them by force only fuels distrust and turns the next visit to the vet into an exhausting battle.

## why
Cooperative care teaches your puppy that physical handling is predictable, respectful, and always brings pleasant consequences. Dogs are not born knowing how to tolerate having their pads held, mouths inspected, or ears examined. If we force physical restraint, they learn to defend themselves with growls or fear-based bites. By breaking touch down into brief one- or two-second contacts rewarded immediately, the puppy learns to relax and cooperate voluntarily.

## steps
1. Always begin during quiet moments, when {name} is resting and calm, never during active play or high arousal.
2. Gently touch one of their front paws for one or two seconds. Release, speak a calm "good" Marker, and deliver a high-value treat.
3. Progress toward the ears and eye area: stroke gently and lift an ear flap for a brief second without squeezing. Mark with "good" and reward.
4. Prepare their muzzle for veterinary exams by resting your hand over their snout and gently lifting a lip to check teeth before rewarding.
5. Practice exam table routines at home on a non-slip mat with brief touches and tasty rewards; if they pull a paw away, respect the pause.

## example
### {name} and clean paws
After a muddy walk, you sit with {name} on the rug with a soft towel and small diced treats. Instead of grabbing a paw and scrubbing hastily, you rest the towel on their paw for a second, mark "good", and deliver a piece of turkey. {name} stays calm, waiting for the next bite. You wipe the next paw, mark, and reward. In three minutes, all four paws are clean and dry without a single struggle or yank.

## compare
### do
Keep touches very brief on paws, eyes, ears, and muzzle, mark with "good", and practice exam handling on a non-slip mat with high-value treats.
### dont
Pin the puppy down by force while they protest. That teaches that handling is a trap to escape from.

## tip
Try it today: gently touch all four paw pads and around the muzzle, one second per area, rewarding after every touch.

## takeaways
- Brief and predictable touch: a couple of seconds on paws, eyes, ears, and mouth before marking and rewarding.
- Cooperative care: if discomfort appears, provide space and lower the difficulty.
- Mock table exams and muzzle handling prepare stress-free vet checkups without struggling.
- Fun, social visits to the vet clinic build positive associations that last a lifetime.
