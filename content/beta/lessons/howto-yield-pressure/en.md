# Giving Way to Leash Pressure

## heading
Teaching your Dog to follow leash guidance without pulling

## hook
Before you even step outside your front door, light leash tension often triggers the exact opposite reaction: your Dog braces against the harness and pulls harder. That opposition reflex is completely natural for any canine, but turns every walk into an exhausting tug-of-war unless you first teach that yielding to leash pressure makes it disappear instantly.

## why
When a dog feels leash tension, their physical instinct is to pull against it to keep balance. Meeting tension with force only triggers more resistance. Steady stillness guides cooperation instead. This 5-minute pre-walk drill builds a completely different habit: light leash pressure is not an invitation to pull, but a tactile communication cue. The moment {name} takes half a step toward the source of pressure, the leash goes completely Slack. That instant release is the primary relief, and marking it with your Marker word locks in the lesson. As taught in the Marking and Rewarding lesson, precise timing within one second makes all the difference.

## steps
1. Inside your living room or hallway, hold the leash at a comfortable distance and apply very light, steady pressure using just two fingers.
2. Keep your hand completely steady without pulling them toward you. Wait quietly until {name} leans or takes a single step in that direction.
3. The instant {name} yields and moves toward you, release all leash tension immediately, mark that step with "good", and deliver a treat.
4. Practice three gentle repetitions to the left and three to the right; if distractions or arousal cause frustration at any point, turn around and end the session calmly.

## example
### {name} in the hallway
You are in the hallway getting ready for the afternoon walk. {name} faces the front door with ears pricked forward. Holding the leash at a relaxed distance, you apply gentle sideways tension using just two fingers, without moving your arm. {name} feels the light resistance and pauses at first. You remain still and silent. Three seconds later, {name} leans their weight toward you and takes half a step. You release the leash instantly, mark with "good", and deliver a treat beside your leg. {name} glances up into your eyes for a spontaneous Check-in: you mark and reward once again.

## compare
### do
Apply gentle, steady pressure with two fingers, waiting patiently for your Dog to take a step toward you before releasing and marking.
### dont
Keep holding leash tension after {name} moves or step toward them to help; waiting to release prevents your Dog from feeling that their step made the pressure disappear.

## tip
Try it today: spend five minutes indoors before your walk practicing three gentle repetitions to the left and three to the right until following light pressure feels effortless.

## takeaways
- Gentle leash pressure is tactile communication, never a physical rope to pull.
- Releasing tension the instant your Dog yields is the relief that teaches the behavior.
- Mark with "good" and deliver the treat the moment {name} takes a step.
- If triggers overwhelm your Dog, turn around, create distance, and end the drill.
