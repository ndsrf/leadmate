# The Walk Begins with the Harness

## heading
Jumping the moment the harness appears

## hook
You unhook the harness and {name} is already bouncing all around you. You try to slip the opening over their head, they dodge, return, and jump against your legs. Eventually you manage to wrestle it on hurriedly just to get out the door. And the walk starts with the exact same frantic energy as the entryway: {name} pulling and you braking from the very first step.

## why
To your Dog, the harness announces the walk—the highlight of the day. That excitement is natural, not deliberate defiance. But what happens in that moment teaches a lasting lesson. If you put it on while jumping occurs, they learn that jumping makes the walk happen sooner. Furthermore, high arousal does not stay behind inside the house: a Dog crossing the threshold already agitated takes much longer to settle and pulls more right from the start. The good news is that the exact same mechanism works in your favor. If the harness only approaches when all four paws are still, and retracts to your chest when jumping starts, they discover that calm is what brings the walk.

## steps
1. Pick up the harness slowly and without party excitement, with small treats ready in your pocket.
2. If jumping occurs, draw the harness back against your chest and wait motionless, without speaking or looking. While jumping continues, nothing happens.
3. If distracted by sniffing or wandering away, say their name once in a soft voice to regain eye contact.
4. When all four paws touch the floor, or they sit and look at you, bring the harness forward. If jumping resumes, the harness returns to your chest.
5. With the harness on, if calm continues, mark that exact instant with a soft "good" Marker and deliver a treat.

## example
### {name} and the entryway hook
It is eight o'clock in the entryway. You take the harness off its hook and {name} starts bouncing, ears forward and tail high. You draw the harness against your chest and wait still. {name} takes two more bounces and begins sniffing the doormat. You speak their name once, softly, and they look up. They sit. You bring the harness forward, and seeing it approach, they pop up abruptly: the harness returns to your chest. On the second attempt, they remain seated. You slide the harness on, they stay still, and you mark with "good" and deliver a treat. Today took three minutes; tomorrow, a little less.

## compare
### do
Draw the harness to your chest when jumping happens and wait still. Once all four paws are on the floor, bring the harness forward again.
### dont
Rush to slip it on while jumping happens, seizing the chance to get out the door. That teaches that jumping brings the walk.

## tip
Try it today: before the walk, count how many times you draw the harness to your chest before successfully putting it on. Note it down and compare tomorrow.

## takeaways
- Jumping does not bring the harness; calm does.
- Doorway arousal follows you onto the walk.
- Harness on and calm: "good" Marker and a treat.
- It takes minutes at first; each day, a little less.
