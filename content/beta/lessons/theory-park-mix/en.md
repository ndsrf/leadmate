# The Busy Park

## heading
Everything stacks up in the park

## hook
You enter the park and {name} does well at first. A jogger passes and eyes stay locked on them. Then a bicycle whizzes by, and a leap hits the end of the leash. When bread crusts appear on the grass, listening stops entirely: neither Leave It nor Let's Go works. Each Distractor on its own is manageable; one right after another, completely overwhelming.

## why
Each Distractor spikes your Dog's Arousal—the internal level of physiological alertness and tension. That Arousal does not vanish the second the jogger leaves; it takes minutes to subside. If another Distractor arrives beforehand, it stacks like water filling a bucket. When the bucket overflows, your Dog crosses their Reactivity Threshold—the point where thinking and listening become physiologically impossible—and reacts. It is not disobedience: it is a nervous system overwhelmed. That is why in a busy park, knowing Cues is not enough. Your job is emptying the bucket between one Distractor and the next.

## steps
1. Before entering, survey the park from the boundary: where joggers and bikes travel, and where quiet pockets remain.
2. Choose the path offering maximum distance. If something fast approaches, gather leash and step aside before your Dog fixates.
3. Address one challenge at a time: fast movement first with "Let's Go"; dropped food second with "Leave It".
4. After each Distractor, take a sniffing pause on the grass. Slow sniffing dissipates Arousal and doubles as a reward.
5. If they refuse treats, cannot hear you, or pull continuously, they have crossed their threshold: turn around and leave the park.

## example
### {name} in the pond park
Saturday morning and you enter the pond park with {name} on the 3-meter leash. A jogger runs past and {name} stiffens with ears pricked forward. You gather leash, cue "Let's Go", and step aside into the grass. There you allow a minute of sniffing at a bush until the tail wags loosely again. Next, a bicycle passes: they look, but take the treat. By a bench, bread lies on the ground; "Leave It", followed by another sniffing pause. After fifteen minutes continuous pulling begins, so you turn for home. Tomorrow, keep it to ten minutes.

## compare
### do
Take a dedicated sniffing pause after each Distractor, stepped off the path. That lets Arousal reset before the next challenge arrives.
### dont
Keep marching continuously through the park because each encounter succeeded in isolation. Arousal continues stacking until the bucket overflows.

## tip
Try it today: on your next park walk, take a one-minute sniffing pause after every jogger, cyclist, or dog that passes by.

## takeaways
- Each Distractor adds Arousal, taking minutes to decline.
- One thing at a time: fast movement first, food second.
- Distance and sniffing pauses empty the bucket.
- If they stop eating or listening, exit the park.
