# Sniffing and Decompression

## heading
Returning from the walk just as restless

## hook
You go on a long, brisk walk, and upon returning home, {name} keeps pacing, mouths whatever is in reach, and refuses to lie down. On the street you barely let them pause: every time a nose nears a tree, you are already walking away. It seems like they need more miles, but what is actually missing is something else: time to sniff.

## why
For your Dog, sniffing is reading the neighborhood: who passed by, when, and where they were headed. That olfactory work engages the mind and dials down arousal: sniffing calmly slows breathing and relaxes the body. That is why a dedicated bout of scent work tires them out more effectively than running, which only winds them up further. We call this decompression: releasing tension brought from home or from a noisy stretch. And because sniffing is intensely motivating, it also acts as a powerful reward: when granted while the leash hangs Slack, it reinforces walking nicely with you.

## steps
1. Choose a quiet spot for sniffing time: a park, an open grassy field, or a quiet street with hedges and light traffic.
2. When the leash hangs Slack, give permission to sniff with a gentle Cue, always the same, such as "Go sniff".
3. Let them choose the direction and the pace. Follow quietly, without tightening the leash or rushing.
4. While they sniff, wait patiently without moving or talking. Spending an entire minute on a single bush is completely normal: do not interrupt.
5. When they lift their head and look back at you, continue walking. Dedicate at least ten minutes of every walk to this.

## example
### {name} and the park hedges
You step outside and find construction work and loud traffic along the avenue. {name} walks with ears pricked forward and the leash goes Taut every few paces. Upon reaching the park hedges, you wait for the leash to hang Slack, and the moment it curves, you whisper "Go sniff". {name} buries a nose into the first hedge and freezes in place. You wait without moving, even as nearly a minute passes. Then they move to another hedge, and another. At first, sniffing is rapid; ten minutes later, they walk slowly, with a low and relaxed tail. Back home, they lie down right away. Tomorrow, relaxation arrives even sooner.

## compare
### do
Wait until the leash hangs Slack, then cue "Go sniff" and accompany them to the hedge. That way, calm is what unlocks scents.
### dont
Allow them to reach the hedge while pulling toward it because "they just want to smell." That teaches that pulling is what earns access to scents.

## tip
Try it today: on a quiet stretch, let your Dog decide the direction for ten minutes. Upon returning, notice whether they settle down faster than other days.

## takeaways
- Sniffing calms and tires them out more than running.
- Sniffing is a reward: it happens on a Slack leash.
- While they sniff, you wait; they decide when it ends.
