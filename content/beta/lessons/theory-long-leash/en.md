# The 3-Meter Long Leash

## heading
Three meters to sniff, and three meters to accelerate

## hook
In the park you give extra leash and {name} wanders happily into the grass to sniff. Suddenly, a jogger appears. {name} takes off sprinting, the three meters snap taut, and the sudden jolt yanks your arm forward. The long leash felt like a gift for both of you, and in a single second it turned into a hazard.

## why
Sniffing is how your Dog reads the world, and doing so at their own pace enables decompression, releasing stored tension. That is why a long leash is a wonderful tool in open spaces. But three meters also function as a runway. If your Dog accelerates from right beside you, they hit the end of the line at top speed and the sudden impact jolts you both. That jolt teaches nothing useful: it only frightens or spikes arousal further. Chasing running objects is instinct, not defiance. With a long leash, your job is proactive Leash Gathering before the sprint begins.

## steps
1. Hold the end loop in one hand and fold the excess into loose accordions in your other palm. Never wrap the leash around your hand or fingers.
2. As they sniff calmly, release folds out to the full three meters. Look up frequently: joggers and cyclists arrive faster than expected.
3. As soon as a fast-moving Distractor appears, perform Leash Gathering fold by fold, hand over hand, bringing the Dog within a meter of you before a sprint starts.
4. If a sprint begins, perform Poste immediately and gather whatever slack remains. The shorter the remaining line, the less momentum builds and the gentler the stop.
5. Step a few paces off the trail and cue "Let's Go". If they look at you, mark with "good" and deliver a treat; once the jogger passes, release the leash again.

## example
### {name} and the pond jogger
Sunday morning walking across the lawn near the pond. {name} sniffs a shrub with the full three meters paid out while you hold the folds in your palm. A jogger appears down the path. {name} lifts their head, ears pricked forward and weight shifted to the front paws. You perform Leash Gathering, hand over hand, and step three paces into the grass. "Let's Go". The first time, hesitation delays turning and the leash goes Taut: you perform Poste and wait. Finally they look back: "good" Marker and a treat. The jogger passes, you release leash, and {name} returns to the shrub. With the next jogger, they turn much sooner.

## compare
### do
Fold excess leash into loose accordion loops inside your palm with the handle loop in the other hand. This lets you gather or release a meter in a second without constriction.
### dont
Wrap the leash around your hand or fingers to shorten quickly. If a sprint after a jogger occurs, the coils snap tight, risking severe hand injury.

## tip
Try it today: at home without your Dog, practice gathering and releasing the long leash ten times fold by fold. Let your hands build muscle memory before heading to the park.

## takeaways
- The long leash provides freedom to sniff and decompress.
- Three meters are also three meters of acceleration runway.
- Fast Distractor in sight: gather leash, step aside, cue "Let's Go".
- Keep loose folds in the palm, never wrapped around the hand.
