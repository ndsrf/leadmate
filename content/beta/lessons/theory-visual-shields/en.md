# Visual Shields and Distance

## heading
Freezing and staring fixedly

## hook
You walk down the sidewalk and {name} spots a scooter approaching, or a dog across the street. They freeze dead in their tracks, ears pricked forward and body rigid. You speak their name and they cannot hear you. A few seconds later barking, pulling, or lunging erupts, and you cannot pinpoint when the walk went off the rails.

## why
Every Dog has a Reactivity Threshold: the distance from a concerning Distractor beyond which thinking becomes impossible. Below threshold, they look, hear you, and take treats. Above threshold, pure reaction takes over. Two factors push them closer to that edge: physical proximity and direct line of sight. That is why staring with Fixation is the critical warning signal: if unbroken, Arousal escalates on its own. It is not defiance or spite; it is fear or intense arousal. A parked car, a dumpster, a hedge, or Handler Shielding breaks their line of sight, and a few extra meters take the pressure off.

## steps
1. Heading out, identify visual shields along your street: parked cars, recycling bins, hedges. Anything that truly blocks line of sight works.
2. As soon as you spot an oncoming Distractor, before Fixation sets in, position a visual shield between you. With fast-moving Distractors like scooters, use whatever is nearest.
3. If no barrier is available, use Handler Shielding: walk on the side facing the Distractor so your body occludes their line of sight.
4. Gain distance: cross to the other sidewalk, turn around, or wait behind the shield until the Distractor passes. When in doubt, add distance.
5. Behind the shield, if they check in or sniff the ground, mark that instant with a soft "good" Marker and deliver a treat. If eating is impossible, move further away.

## example
### {name} and the market street scooter
Walking down the market street sidewalk, you hear a scooter approaching along the road. {name} lifts their head and pricks ears forward. Before Fixation locks in, you take two paces and step behind a parked car. {name} cranes a neck to peer around, but the vehicle blocks line of sight. The moment they look back at you, you speak a gentle "good" Marker and deliver a treat. The scooter glides past and you resume walking. The first time, taking the treat takes a moment; by the third, they turn toward you upon hearing the sound.

## compare
### do
The moment you spot what concerns them, before Fixation takes hold, create distance: turn around or step behind a parked car. Then, "good" Marker and a treat.
### dont
Stand frozen in the middle of the sidewalk waiting for them to "get used to it" while staring intently. That allows Arousal to climb until barking or lunging erupts.

## tip
Try it today: on your regular walk, locate three visual shields you could use, like a car, a bin, or a hedge. Just spot them, without needing to use them yet.

## takeaways
- If they cannot see it, staying connected with you is far easier.
- Cars, bins, hedges, and Handler Shielding block line of sight.
- Act before Fixation locks in.
- When in doubt, add distance: cross the street or turn around.
