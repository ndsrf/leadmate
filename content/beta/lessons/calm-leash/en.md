# The Basics of a Calm Walk

## heading
A calm walk begins before leaving home

## hook
You pick up the leash and {name} is already spinning in the hallway. You open the door, they bolt out like a rocket, and the first few minutes turn into a tug-of-war: you try to slow down while {name} tries to reach everything. By the time you return, you both have less energy than when you started. It is not that your Dog cannot walk nicely: the walk started in overdrive, and no one has taught another pace.

## why
Your Dog learns from what works. If the door opens every time they jump, jumping becomes the way outside. If pulling moves you forward, pulling becomes the way to walk. Furthermore, arousal accumulates: a Dog who leaves the house already agitated takes much longer to settle and reacts more intensely to everything encountered. That is why the first two minutes set the tone for the entire walk. The good news is that the same mechanism works in your favor: if calm is what opens the door and moves you forward, your Dog will choose calm more and more.

## steps
1. Before picking up the leash, slow your own pace: move steadily and speak in a soft, low tone without party excitement.
2. Clip the leash and wait by the door. Open it only when all four paws are on the floor; if they rush forward, calmly close it and wait again.
3. Step out together and maintain a steady, calm stride, without sudden accelerations or abrupt direction changes.
4. Let the leash hang in a relaxed J-shape: Slack, with the curve close to you. That Slack is what you want to maintain.
5. If the leash goes Taut, come to a complete stop and wait. As soon as it goes Slack, continue walking: moving with you is what moves them forward.

## example
### {name} and the front door
It is eight o'clock and {name} is already whining by the front door. You put on your jacket calmly and clip the leash without speaking. You place your hand on the doorknob and {name} jumps. You remove your hand and wait. Within seconds they sit, and the door opens. On the sidewalk, they pull toward the corner tree: you stop. {name} looks back, the leash goes Slack, and you move forward. On day one, reaching the corner takes five minutes. A week later, barely one.

## compare
### do
Wait with your hand on the doorknob until all four paws are on the floor, then open. Calm is the key.
### dont
Open the door while they jump or whine "so they can calm down outside." That teaches that jumping opens the door.

## tip
Try it today: count how many times the leash goes Taut in the first five minutes. You do not need to correct anything; just notice your starting point.

## takeaways
- Calm is what opens the door and keeps you moving forward.
- Keep the leash in a J-shape: Slack, with the curve close to you.
- Leash Taut, you stop; leash Slack, you go.
