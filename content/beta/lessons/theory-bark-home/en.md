# Barking at Home

## heading
Barking whenever anything happens

## hook
A neighbor walks past the landing and {name} barks. The elevator dings, a bark. Spotting another dog through the window, barking again. You yell "quiet!" and they bark even more. Barking is exhausting, and neighbors are starting to complain. There is a calm way to turn down the volume, and it starts with understanding why barking occurs.

## why
Barking is how a Dog communicates: to alert, express excitement, demand attention, or relieve boredom. Every time barking happens and something goes away (the neighbor walks off, you pay attention), they learn that barking works. If you yell, to them it feels as though you are barking along, and arousal spikes further. What truly reduces barking is removing the cause, stopping the payoff for noise, and rewarding quiet. If barking, howling, or destruction occurs only when you leave, it could be separation anxiety: consult a professional.

## steps
1. Track when barking occurs: at the window, at the doorbell, while you sit on the sofa, or when left alone. Each reason has its own solution.
2. Remove the visual Distractor whenever possible: close the curtain or cover the lower glass, and offer a resting spot far from the window.
3. If nonstop barking is underway, wait without speaking or making eye contact until a pause occurs. Adding your voice or shouting only winds them up further.
4. As soon as a quiet pause happens, say "good" in a soft voice and deliver a treat. Over the days, lengthen the quiet pause before rewarding.
5. If barking is for attention, do not look, speak, or touch them. When silence falls or they lie down, offer attention and praise.
6. Provide mental enrichment: a food-stuffed toy, walks with ample sniffing time, and daily play. A Dog with an occupied mind barks far less.

## example
### {name} and the living room window
Every afternoon the mail carrier walks by and {name} barks from the sofa. One afternoon you pull the curtain halfway and place a mat far from the window. When the mail carrier is heard, they stand up, but do not bark; you wait a few seconds, and the moment quiet holds, you say "good" and deliver a treat. The first day is challenging; by the third, they sit and look back at you expecting the reward.

## compare
### do
Remove the visual Distractor (draw the curtain, relocate to another room) and reward pauses of silence, even two-second ones.
### dont
Yell "quiet!" or hand over a treat while barking: the former adds noise and the latter reinforces the bark.

## tip
Today: count how many seconds of silence {name} manages before the first reward and note it down. Tomorrow aim for one more second.

## takeaways
- Barking happens for a reason: find it.
- Remove the Distractor before training.
- Reward silence, even when brief.
- Shouting adds noise: wait calmly.
