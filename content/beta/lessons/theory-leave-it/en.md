# Leave It

## heading
Spotting food on the sidewalk and lunging for it

## hook
You are walking along the sidewalk and {name} stops dead in their tracks: a discarded sandwich crust lies by the trash can. Before you can react, their nose is already right over it. You try to steer their head away, repeating "leave it, leave it," while {name} frantically gulps it down as fast as possible. Tomorrow, they scan the pavement with even greater determination.

## why
To your Dog, food on the ground is a jackpot found independently: nobody has to grant permission. That is why scavenging happens; it is not bad manners. If you dash in to snatch it away, they learn something dangerous: eat as quickly as possible before hands arrive. And shouting a word when they are already over the food communicates nothing, because no one has taught what that sound means. "Leave It" succeeds if, beforehand at home, they discovered that turning away from food earns something far tastier from your hand. Then, out on the street, choosing to check in with you beats the pavement jackpot.

## steps
1. At home, close a plain piece of kibble inside your fist and let them sniff. When they turn their muzzle away, say "good" and deliver a high-value treat from the other hand.
2. Once they back away the moment your fist appears, say "Leave It" just before presenting your hand. The Cue begins to mean: turn your nose away, and I pay the reward.
3. Increase difficulty gradually: place the treat on the floor covered by your hand; then uncovered; then walking past on leash.
4. On the street, speak "Leave It" once in a normal tone the second they spot food: ears forward, head dropping. Always before they arrive.
5. When their head turns toward you, speak "good", deliver a treat from your hand, and walk away from the food. The reward never comes from the ground.
6. If you are late and the object is already in their mouth, trade calmly: present a higher-value treat right at their muzzle. Once dropped, pick up the item yourself.

## example
### {name} and the newsstand sandwich
You walk past the newsstand and {name} spots a sandwich crust a few paces ahead by the trash can. Ears prick forward and neck extends. Before they accelerate, you say "Leave It" once. The first time, hesitation: they take another step and sniff the air. You wait still on a Slack leash. Then they pivot their head toward you: "good", a treat from your hand, and you continue walking away from the sandwich. Two blocks down there is a wrapper on the sidewalk, and this time they turn the instant the Cue is spoken.

## compare
### do
Speak "Leave It" once the second food is spotted while still several paces away. If they turn toward you, mark "good" and reward from your hand.
### dont
Repeat "leave it, leave it" when their nose is already buried in it. It comes too late, offers no guidance, and degrades the value of the Cue.

## tip
Try it today: before the walk, do five repetitions at home with a closed fist. Note how many seconds it takes to back away from your hand.

## takeaways
- Food on the ground rewards itself; your treat must beat it.
- Start at home with plain kibble; street trials come later.
- "Leave It" once, the moment they spot it, before arrival.
- Turning toward you earns the treat; never eat from the floor.
