# Blind Corners

## heading
Suddenly, a bicycle around the corner

## hook
You walk along the sidewalk and {name} is a meter ahead. They reach the corner before you do and, right as they turn, a bicycle appears inches from their nose. They flinch, bark, or lunge, and you only feel the yank. Neither you nor {name} saw it coming.

## why
What startles your Dog is not so much what appears, but how close and sudden it is. Distance provides time: at ten meters, a bicycle is something to watch; inches away, it is a threat to defend against. A blind corner, occluded by a wall or dense hedge, erases that distance. Furthermore, dogs often hear or scent oncoming Distractors before you do, arriving at the corner already on high alert. If they are walking out ahead on a Taut leash, they face the surprise first and without your support. Turning slowly, by your side, and far from the wall restores that safety buffer.

## steps
1. As soon as you spot a blind corner, begin slowing down three or four paces before reaching it, not right at the turn.
2. Say their name once in a soft voice. When they return to your side on a Slack leash, deliver a treat at hip height.
3. Take a wide arc: move away from the wall and round the corner along the widest part of the sidewalk, without stepping into the street.
4. Turn slowly and together. That way you see around the corner before your Dog does, and anything approaching meets you from several meters away.
5. If a Distractor appears and your Dog stays calm, mark with a soft "good" Marker and deliver a treat. If tension spikes, turn around and walk away.

## example
### {name} and the pharmacy corner
You are heading home and spot the pharmacy corner with its tall wall. {name} walks ahead with ears pricked forward: hearing something on the cross street. Three steps beforehand you begin slowing down and speak their name softly. The first time takes a moment, but they come to your side and receive a treat by your hip. You swing wide of the wall and turn through the open arc of the sidewalk. A bicycle rides down the cross street and you spot it meters away. {name} looks at it, then checks in with you: "good", and another treat. At the next corner, they join your side even faster.

## compare
### do
Slow down a few paces early, bring your Dog to your side with a treat, and take a wide arc on the sidewalk slowly on a Slack leash.
### dont
Hug the wall tightly with your Dog out ahead to keep moving fast. That sets them up to meet whatever appears head-on, inches away.

## tip
Try it today: choose two corners on your block with good visibility and practice slowing down early and gathering your Dog by your side. Save blind corners for later.

## takeaways
- What frightens is proximity and surprise.
- Slow down three or four steps early, not at the corner.
- Turn with your Dog by your side on a Slack leash.
- Walking the wide arc gives you early line of sight from a distance.
