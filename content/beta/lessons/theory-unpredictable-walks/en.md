# Unpredictable Walks

## heading
When walks are predictable, your Dog tunes out

## hook
You walk through the front door, cross the exact same street, and head toward the park in a straight line at the same daily pace. {name} walks three paces ahead, mind wandering across distant smells. There is no major conflict or pulling match, but there is zero engagement either: walking on autopilot while you act as nothing more than an anchor behind the leash.

## why
Dogs thrive on patterns and predictability. When every corner and sidewalk path is completely familiar, {name} has no reason to pay attention: the routine navigates the way, leaving attention free to wander toward exciting outdoor distractions. An Unpredictable Walk breaks that autopilot habit. By suddenly altering your direction, changing your walking pace, or pausing without speaking, you encourage your Dog to check where you are going. Every spontaneous glance back toward you is a Check-in: the moment they choose your company over the environment. The structured cue "Let's Go" marks connected movement together, while "Go Sniff" releases your Dog to explore freely.

## steps
1. Walk ten paces in a straight line, then calmly turn 180 degrees and head in the opposite direction without jerking the leash.
2. As soon as {name} turns to follow your movement and offers a spontaneous Check-in, mark that instant with a soft "good" and deliver a treat beside your leg.
3. Mix in sudden pace changes: switch from a slow stroll to several brisk steps, or pause entirely for three seconds without saying a word.
4. Cue "Let's Go" to resume structured walking beside you, and cue "Go Sniff" whenever you reach a safe grassy spot where investigating scents is welcome.

## example
### {name} and the town square
Walking across the paved town square, {name} heads toward a flock of pigeons in the distance with the leash lightly curving. Instead of walking straight ahead, you make a sharp ninety-degree left turn and quicken your pace by three strides. {name} senses the shift in momentum, turns around, and looks directly at your face. You mark that Check-in with a gentle "good" and deliver a treat right beside your knee. You cue "Let's Go" to continue, make two more random turns between the park benches, and finally cue "Go Sniff" when reaching the grass to release exploration.

## compare
### do
Change direction or pace quietly and reward every spontaneous Check-in the moment your Dog chooses to turn toward you.
### dont
Chatter constantly or repeat "Let's Go" at every turn; talking continuously lets your Dog stay on autopilot instead of looking to see where you are going.

## tip
Try it today: during the next ten minutes of your walk, make five unexpected right-angle or U-turns without speaking. Mark and reward each voluntary glance.

## takeaways
- Predictable routes create autopilot walks; unpredictability rebuilds voluntary focus.
- Every voluntary glance toward you is a Check-in worth marking and rewarding.
- "Let's Go" frames structured walking while "Go Sniff" unlocks free sniffing time.
- Turn without leash pops: let the sudden change of direction prompt the choice.
