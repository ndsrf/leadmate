# Unpredictable Walks

## heading
When walks are predictable, your Dog tunes out

## hook
You walk through the front door, cross the exact same street, and head toward the park in a straight line at the same daily pace. {name} walks beside you on a loose leash, mind wandering across distant smells. There is no major conflict or pulling match, but there is zero engagement either: walking on autopilot while you act as nothing more than an anchor behind the leash.

## why
Dogs learn through patterns and anticipation. When every corner and sidewalk path is completely familiar, {name} has no reason to pay attention: the routine navigates the way, leaving attention free to wander toward exciting outdoor distractions. An Unpredictable Walk breaks that autopilot habit. By altering your direction, changing your walking pace, or pausing without speaking, you encourage your Dog to check where you are going. Every spontaneous glance back toward you is a Check-in: the moment they choose your company over the environment. The structured cue "Let's Go" frames connected movement together, while "Go Sniff" releases your Dog to explore freely.

## steps
1. Walk ten paces in a straight line with the leash hanging Slack. Slow your pace slightly, then smoothly turn 180 degrees so the leash stays Slack and never tightens suddenly; if {name} ever fixates on a distraction, create distance, turn away, or end the session.
2. As soon as {name} turns to follow your movement and offers a spontaneous Check-in, mark that instant with a soft "good" and deliver a treat beside your leg.
3. Mix in sudden pace changes: switch from a slow stroll to several brisk steps, or pause entirely for three seconds without saying a word.
4. Cue "Let's Go" to resume structured walking beside you, and cue "Go Sniff" whenever you reach a safe grassy spot where investigating scents is welcome.

## example
### {name} in the town square
Walking across the town square on a Slack leash, {name} notices distant pigeons. Before attention locks on, you slow down and turn smoothly to the left, keeping the curve loose. Sensing your direction change, {name} turns and glances at your face. You mark that Check-in with "good" and deliver a treat beside your leg. You cue "Let's Go" to resume walking, make two unhurried turns, and cue "Go Sniff" at the grass to release exploration.

## compare
### do
Change direction or pace quietly and reward every spontaneous Check-in the moment your Dog chooses to turn toward you.
### dont
Chatter constantly or announce every turn out loud; when your voice warns of each change, your Dog stays on autopilot instead of looking to see where you are going.

## tip
Try it today: during the next ten minutes of your walk, make five unexpected right-angle or U-turns without speaking. Mark and reward each voluntary glance.

## takeaways
- Predictable routes create autopilot walks; unpredictability rebuilds voluntary focus.
- Every voluntary glance toward you is a Check-in worth marking and rewarding.
- "Let's Go" frames structured walking while "Go Sniff" unlocks free sniffing time.
- Turn on a Slack leash: let the change of direction prompt the voluntary choice.
