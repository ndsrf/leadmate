# The Smile Leash

## heading
Walking nicely, and nobody notices

## hook
You already know to stop when the leash goes Taut. But notice the rest of the walk: {name} walks ten meters by your side on a hanging leash, while you are lost in thought. Nobody says a word. The moment pulling happens, however, you stop and all your attention turns to {name}. Without intending to, the best moments pass by unrewarded.

## why
Your Dog repeats what pays off. Stopping when pulling occurs ensures pulling does not work, but it does not teach where the value lies. For that, you have to reinforce what you want to see. The "smile" is easy to spot: the leash hangs in a gentle Slack curve, lower than your hand and the harness, because neither of you is pulling. It does not matter whether the curve sits closer to you, like the J-shape from day one: what matters is that it hangs Slack. And the delivery spot matters just as much as the timing. If the treat always appears right beside your leg, that becomes the most rewarding spot on the entire walk.

## steps
1. Before heading out, fill the pocket on your Dog's side with small, soft treats, roughly pea-sized.
2. As you walk, glance down at the leash from time to time. When it hangs in a smile, say "good" in a soft voice: that Marker signals the exact moment you appreciate.
3. Lower the treat down to your trouser seam at mouth level, and let them take it right beside your leg.
4. At first, reward generously: a smile leash every few paces. Once maintained effortlessly, space out the treats gradually, without cutting them abruptly.
5. If the smile flattens into a straight, Taut line, stop just like day one. When it hangs Slack again, resume walking and reward the next smile.

## example
### {name} on the market street
It is Saturday morning and you are walking down the market street. The leash hangs in a gentle smile and {name} sniffs the curb by your side. You say "good" and deliver a treat at the seam of your trousers; they eat it and glance up. Four paces later, another smile, another "good". Outside the greengrocer a pigeon appears, the leash stretches straight, and you stop. It takes a moment, but they turn toward you and the leash hangs Slack once more. You continue, and the next smile is rewarded right beside your leg. After ten minutes, {name} spends far more time beside you than out ahead.

## compare
### do
Bring the treat down to your leg at mouth level so they come beside you to take it. That makes the best spot on the walk right beside you.
### dont
Feed with an outstretched arm out in front, or drop treats ahead of your path. That teaches that good things are ahead, encouraging pulling forward.

## tip
Try it today: put ten treats in your pocket before leaving and spend them all on smile leashes during the first five minutes of the walk.

## takeaways
- The smile is a hanging leash: neither of you is pulling.
- Stopping removes the payoff from pulling; rewarding the smile teaches where to be.
- Deliver the reward right beside your leg, never out in front.
- Reward frequently at first, then space out rewards gradually.
