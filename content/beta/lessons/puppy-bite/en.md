# Bite Inhibition and Needle Teeth

## heading
Sharp teeth and hands during play

## hook
You are playing on the floor with {name} and, out of nowhere, those needle-sharp teeth clamp into your fingers or sleeve. It genuinely hurts. You gasp "ouch" or try to jerk your hand away, but {name} gets even more excited and lunges to bite again like prey trying to escape.

## why
Mouthing everything is a puppy's natural way of exploring the world and soothing sore gums while teething. They do not bite out of malice or dominance: they bite because they are learning how much force their jaws possess. In the litter, siblings yelp and stop playing if someone bites too hard. If you move your hands quickly, shout, or struggle, you turn your fingers into an interactive toy. The key is teaching two concepts: skin is hypersensitive, and all fun ends instantly the second teeth touch skin.

## steps
1. Always keep appropriate chew toys within reach where your puppy spends the day. When they approach wanting to bite, offer the toy before teeth touch skin.
2. If teeth brush against your hand or clothing, freeze completely still, without speaking or struggling. Moving your hand quickly triggers the chase instinct.
3. If biting persists with high arousal, use a reverse timeout: stand up calmly, turn your back, or step out of the room closing the door for 20 to 30 seconds.
4. Practice trading: if they grab an off-limits object (such as a slipper), never pry their jaws open by force. Offer a high-value treat or favorite toy in exchange.
5. When they choose to chew their own toy peacefully, praise them with a soft "good" Marker and let them concentrate on the toy.

## example
### {name} and the hallway slippers
You are walking down the hallway and {name} lunges at the hem of your pants with needle teeth. Instead of shaking your leg or scolding, you freeze completely like a statue. {name} releases the fabric seeing that it does not move. In that exact instant, you pull a rope toy from your pocket and wiggle it along the floor. {name} grabs the rope with enthusiasm. You say "good" and play for two minutes with the toy. You taught what is acceptable to chew without any frustration.

## compare
### do
Freeze still if teeth touch skin or clothing, always redirect to appropriate chew toys, and calmly leave the room if arousal escalates.
### dont
Yank hands away quickly, yell, push them away, or pry jaws open by force. That only increases arousal and struggling.

## tip
Try it today: place three safe chew toys around the living room and keep your hands relaxed like a statue next time they try to play with your fingers.

## takeaways
- Skin and clothing are never toys.
- A reverse timeout teaches that biting ends attention.
- Trading for better items prevents resource guarding.
- No physical punishment: puppies need to learn jaw inhibition with calm.
