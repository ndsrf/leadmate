# Waiters and People Passing By

## heading
A waiter walks by and {name} jumps up

## hook
You are sitting on the terrace and {name} is resting on their mat. Suddenly a waiter walks by with a tray, or someone pushes a stroller, and {name} jumps up. The long leash lies across the walkway and the waiter has to step over it. Your stomach tightens, and {name} is already pulling toward the tray.

## why
On a terrace everything passes within a metre of your Dog: trays, chairs, children and other dogs. If the leash is long and tight, {name} reaches the walkway in two strides and every passer-by becomes an opportunity. A short but slack leash lets {name} lie down calmly and gives you control without pulling. With {name} on the wall side and your body between them and the passage, they see less and get less worked up. And when {name} looks back at you as someone passes, that is their own decision, and you can reward it. If it is too much, there is always an exit: move to another table or leave.

## steps
1. When you sit down, put {name} on the wall or corner side, with your chair between them and the walkway.
2. Gather the leash until it is short, but always slack, with a soft curve. No jerks and no tension.
3. When you see someone coming, place your body and legs between {name} and the passage, like a calm shield.
4. If {name} looks back at you as the waiter passes, say "good" and drop a treat on the mat.
5. If {name} gets restless or stands up, move them away from the passage, change seats or ask for another table. Leaving is also a good option.

## example
### {name} and the waiter with the tray
You are on a terrace with {name} on the mat beside the wall. You hear a waiter approaching with a tray. You gather the leash, short and slack, and put your legs between {name} and the walkway. {name} lifts their head and watches the tray. You wait. As the waiter passes, {name} looks back at you. You say "good" and drop a treat on the mat. The first time takes some effort, but by the third time another waiter passes and {name} hardly turns an ear.

## compare
### do
A short but slack leash, {name} on the wall side and your body between them and the walkway. When they look back as someone passes, "good" and a treat on the mat.
### dont
Leaving the leash long across the walkway and {name} on the passage side. Every waiter becomes a jolt for both of you, and that is not {name}'s fault.

## tip
Try it today: on your next visit, before you order, pick the wall spot, put {name} behind your chair and keep the leash short and slack.

## takeaways
- Wall on one side, your body toward the walkway.
- Short but slack leash, never tight.
- Reward on the mat when they look back as someone passes.
- If it is hard, change tables, shorten the visit or leave.
