# Marking and Rewarding

## heading
You give rewards, but your Dog doesn't know why

## hook
{name} sits at the curb and it looks perfect. You reach for a treat in your pocket, pull it out, bend down… and by the time it reaches their mouth, they have already stood up to sniff a lamppost. You rewarded them, yes, but to {name} the reward came for sniffing the lamppost. You paid for a different moment than intended.

## why
Your Dog connects the reward to whatever they were doing immediately before receiving it, in the final second. Pulling out a treat takes longer than that, so you often end up paying for something else. Marking solves this: marking means saying a short word, always the same, at the exact instant they do what you want. Here we use a gentle "good" as our Marker word. It means "that, right now" and signals that a reward is on its way, so the reward can arrive a moment later without losing its meaning. But a Marker only works if it keeps its promise: if you say "good" and nothing follows, it ceases to mean anything.

## steps
1. At home, before the walk, say "good" in a soft voice and immediately give a small, soft treat. Repeat ten times without asking for anything.
2. Pick just one behavior for today's walk: looking at you, keeping the leash Slack, or sitting at the curb.
3. Say "good" the instant it happens, keeping your hands still. First the Marker word; only then does your hand reach into your pocket.
4. Deliver the reward promptly within one to two seconds: a treat right beside your leg, moving forward, or letting them sniff. Every "good" carries its reward.
5. If you miss the moment, do not mark late: wait for the next opportunity. A few well-timed marks teach far more than many mistimed ones.

## example
### {name} and the bus stop tree
You walk along the sidewalk toward the bus stop on a Slack leash. {name} sniffs around the base of a tree and lifts their head toward you, but by the time you open your mouth they are already back to the ground: you stay quiet and keep walking. Half a minute later, they look at you again. In that very instant you say "good" with your hands still, then draw a treat and deliver it beside your leg. A few steps later, they look back at you again. Today you catch four check-ins before reaching the stop; tomorrow, even more.

## compare
### do
Say "good" with hands still at the exact instant, and only then reach for the treat. The word marks the moment; the reward can follow a second later.
### dont
Reach into your pocket before marking. {name} ends up watching your hand rather than what they are doing, and the Marker arrives late.

## tip
Try it today: in the first five minutes of the walk, say "good" every time they look at you and reward immediately after. Count how many check-ins you catch.

## takeaways
- "Good" means "that, right now."
- First the word; then the hand and the reward.
- Every "good" carries its reward, always.
- If you are late, do not mark: wait for the next time.
