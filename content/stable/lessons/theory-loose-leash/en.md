# Walking on a Slack Leash

## heading
Every walk is a tug-of-war

## hook
You step outside with {name} and within two minutes your shoulder aches. Pulling toward the tree, the lamppost, the dog across the street. You trail behind, braking and yielding, and when they finally walk nicely beside you, you barely notice: you are bracing for the next yank. Upon returning home, you both have less energy than if you had gone for a run.

## why
To your Dog, the outside world is overflowing with rewards: rich scents, novel sights, forward momentum. Whatever behavior occurs immediately prior to reaching those rewards is what gets reinforced. If pulling reaches the tree, pulling worked. If you only speak up when pulling happens, the calm stretches pass by unreinforced. And if a surprise Distractor, like a skateboard, appears close by, arousal spikes instantly and lunging becomes the default reflex. Walking on a Slack leash flips the equation: the world opens up only when the leash hangs Slack, and you spot challenging situations before your Dog does.

## steps
1. Scan far ahead, half a block down the street. That way you spot a skateboarder, another dog, or a cat before your Dog does, giving you time to respond.
2. If a challenging Distractor approaches, step aside or slow down before the leash goes Taut. When in doubt, create more distance.
3. Mark the good moments: when walking on a Slack leash or disengaging from a Distractor to check in with you, speak "good" and deliver a treat.
4. If the leash goes Taut, perform Poste: stop still without jerking and wait. As soon as it goes Slack, continue walking.
5. Pay with the environment: after a stretch on a Slack leash, cue "Go sniff" and accompany them over to that tree they wanted to investigate.

## example
### {name} and the skateboard
You walk down the market street and {name} walks on a Slack leash. You look far ahead and spot a boy on a skateboard approaching in the distance. {name} has not seen it yet. You step aside toward a shop window before it arrives. {name} hears the wheels, pricks ears forward, and the leash stretches: you perform Poste. Watching the skateboard pass, the moment their head turns back toward you: "good" Marker and a treat. You resume walking. Ten meters ahead is the tree they always want to smell; having walked nicely without pulling, you cue "Go sniff". The first block is challenging; the next, a little easier.

## compare
### do
Perform Poste a few paces before the tree. Once the leash hangs Slack, cue "Go sniff": you arrive together and the scent rewards the Slack leash.
### dont
Let them pull all the way to the tree because "they were going to sniff it anyway." That scent reinforces the pull, ensuring they pull even harder next time.

## tip
Try it today: before reaching the first corner, mark and reward five moments of a Slack leash. Notice how many used to pass by unnoticed.

## takeaways
- Whatever pays for the walk is what gets learned.
- Scan far ahead: spot difficult Distractors first.
- Mark Slack leash moments, not just the pulls.
- Sniffing is the ultimate reward for a Slack leash.
