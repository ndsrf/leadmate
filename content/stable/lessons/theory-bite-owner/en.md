# If Your Dog Bites You

## heading
Your Dog bites and you don't know what to do

## hook
You are playing with {name} and suddenly teeth clamp down on your hand. Or you try to take away a chew and a growl rumbles. Or you push them off the sofa and get bitten. It hurts, it is frightening, and it is hard not to shout. Feeling that way is completely normal. Before moving forward, remember one critical principle: safety comes first, and understanding why biting happens comes next.

## why
A Dog does not bite out of malice or to dominate you. Biting happens because something is wrong: overarousal during play with no off-switch, guarding a valued resource (food, a toy, a favorite spot), fear, physical pain, or frustration. Very often, warning signs preceded the bite: stiffening, growling, or avoiding contact. If you punish the warning, they learn not to warn, and the next bite lands without notice. That is why the priority is managing the situation, not winning a power struggle.

## steps
1. Place a door or baby gate between {name} and children, visitors, or other pets. No one should be left unattended with a Dog that has bitten.
2. Note when biting occurs: during play, around food, on the sofa, when picked up, or when startled awake. Patterns reveal what situations to avoid for now.
3. Respect warning signs: if their body stiffens, a growl rumbles, or they avoid you, step back and grant space. Never scold a warning.
4. Prevent situations that spark biting: provide meals and high-value chews in another room behind a closed door, and trade with a treat rather than physically pushing them off furniture.
5. If biting happens during play, end the session in silence: stand up, cross your arms, and step out of the room for a few moments. Upon returning, offer a toy instead of your hands.
6. Reward what you want to see: a treat when resting calmly or playing gently with a toy. What gets rewarded gets repeated.

## example
### {name} and the sofa chew
Saturday afternoon in the living room. {name} is chewing on a bone on the sofa, and as you approach, their body stiffens and a growl rumbles. Instead of snatching it away, you step back and toss a treat across the room away from the sofa. When they hop down to eat it, you calmly remove the bone and put it away. From that day on, high-value chews are given on their bed, undisturbed behind a closed door. Over weeks the growling subsides, but you record every incident to consult with a veterinary behaviorist.

## compare
### do
Step back, give {name} space, and seek help from a veterinary behaviorist if biting has occurred with force or repeatedly.
### dont
Scold, punish, or overpower them when a growl occurs: that teaches them not to warn, and the next bite may arrive without notice.

## tip
Today: note on your phone the last three times biting or growling occurred, with the time and what was happening right before. This will help you avoid them and assist the professional. To practice calm with hands nearby, play "Calm Hands and Leash" in World 1: it is calm practice, not bite training.

## takeaways
- Safety for everyone comes first.
- A growl is a warning: respect it.
- Avoid situations that spark biting.
- Seek professional help if biting happens with force or frequency.
