# The Slack U-Leash

## heading
The leash tightens without warning

## hook
You head out with the leash hanging Slack and everything is smooth. A few paces later, you feel the yank down your arm without knowing when it began. You perform Poste, it goes Slack, you continue, and moments later it repeats. It feels like tension arrives out of nowhere, but that is rarely the case: the leash gives you early warnings, if you know what to watch for.

## why
Your Dog walks faster than you and the street is rich with enticing scents. When something sparks interest, pace quickens gradually, and the leash transforms. First it hangs in a deep, relaxed U-shape: you are moving in sync. Then the curve shortens into a J-shape, still Slack but close to your hand: your Dog is surging ahead. Finally, straight and Taut. During the J-shape they can still hear you, because attention is not yet fully locked onto the scent. Once the leash goes Taut, they are fixated and disengaging takes far more effort. That is why you want to act during the J-shape. And not with your arm: reaching forward to avoid tension maintains the illusion of a U, but teaches that surging ahead works.

## steps
1. Keep your leash hand still against your waist and glance at the leash regularly, not just at your Dog.
2. Read its shape: deep U-shape, walking together; J-shape, surging ahead; straight line, pulling has begun and it is time for Poste.
3. The moment you spot the J-shape, make an adjustment before tension hits: slow your pace slightly or speak their name softly once.
4. If they keep advancing, calmly turn 180 degrees and walk in the opposite direction. When they follow, the U-shape returns naturally.
5. As soon as they return to your side, mark that exact instant with a gentle "good" Marker and deliver a treat beside your leg.

## example
### {name} and the sidewalk flowerbeds
You walk along the garden sidewalk past flowerbeds and a bench. {name} catches the scent of blossoms, accelerates, and the U flattens into a J-shape. You slow your pace and speak their name softly once. This time they do not hear you and keep pulling toward the flowers, so you calmly turn 180 degrees and walk the other way. {name} turns to follow, the leash drops back into a U-shape, and they reach your leg: "good" Marker and a treat. Near the bench another J appears; you say their name and they check in on the first try.

## compare
### do
Keep your hand steady by your waist. If the U closes into a J-shape, adjust yourself: slow down, speak their name, or turn around.
### dont
Stretch your arm forward to keep the leash from going Taut. The U appears preserved, but your hand followed them and they learned to forge ahead.

## tip
Try it today: on a stretch of your street, count how many times you spot the J-shape before the leash goes Taut. Every J you catch is one less yank.

## takeaways
- U, in sync; J, forging ahead; straight, already pulling.
- Act on the J-shape: your Dog can still listen.
- The U-shape comes from proximity, not reaching your arm.
- When they return to your side: "good" Marker and a treat by your leg.
