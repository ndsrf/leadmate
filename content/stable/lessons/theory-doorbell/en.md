# The Doorbell and the Mat

## heading
Bolting to the door whenever the chime rings

## hook
The doorbell rings and {name} charges down the hallway, barking, claws skidding on the floor. You trail behind shouting for quiet, crack the door as best you can, and the guest enters amidst jumping paws. By the time calm finally settles, ten minutes have evaporated. And next time the chime rings, the entire spectacle repeats.

## why
To your Dog, the doorbell is not merely background noise: it reliably heralds intense excitement—a visitor, a package, someone smelling of the outside world. This association was forged through countless repetitions, just as you recognize your ringtone. It is not disobedience; it is anticipation and conditioned habit. If you also dash and raise your voice, the atmosphere grows even more frantic. The solution is creating a new association: the chime cues heading to the mat, where relaxing quietly earns the payoff. A Dog lying calmly on a mat physically cannot be charging the front door.

## steps
1. First, practice without the doorbell: cue "to your mat", guide them there, and deliver a treat once they lie down. Repeat over several days until they go independently.
2. Play a recorded doorbell chime on your phone at very low volume. As soon as it sounds, or when they fixate on the door, cue "to your mat" once.
3. If they reach the mat but remain standing or restless, wait silently. If distracted by sniffing, say their name once in a soft voice.
4. When they lie down and relax their body, mark that exact instant with a soft "good" Marker and deliver the treat right on the mat.
5. Increase the volume gradually and, later on, ask a friend to ring the actual doorbell. If going to the mat fails, take a step back.
6. While training progresses, manage real visits by keeping your Dog in another room with a long-lasting chew. This prevents practicing the charge to the door.

## example
### {name} and Sunday's doorbell chime
Sunday afternoon in the hallway with your phone in hand and treats in your pocket. You play the recorded chime at low volume. {name} pricks ears forward and locks eyes on the front door. Before barking erupts, you cue "to your mat" and they head over. They stand whimpering with weight shifted forward: you wait silently. They sniff the edge of the mat; you speak their name softly and they look up. Shortly after, they lie down and shift their hip onto its side. "Good"—and a treat lands on the mat. The first trial takes two minutes; by the fifth, barely twenty seconds.

## compare
### do
Wait silently until they lie down and relax their body, then mark. If they stand and head for the door, send them back to the mat.
### dont
Deliver the treat the second paws touch the mat while they still stand, whimper, and stare at the door. That rewards arousal, not calm.

## tip
Try it today: play a recorded chime on your phone at very low volume, and spot your Dog's very first alert signal: ears, door gaze, or bark. That is your moment to act.

## takeaways
- The doorbell signals visitors: that is why dogs run, not from defiance.
- At the first warning, send to the mat before arousal spikes.
- Restless, wait; lying down and relaxed, mark and reward.
- Start quietly and build up gradually; if struggling, step back.
