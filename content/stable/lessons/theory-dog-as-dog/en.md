# A Dog, Not a Plush Toy

## heading
Loving your Dog like a baby

## hook
You scoop them up in your arms when they whimper, let them sleep in your bed, and shower their face with kisses. You do it out of genuine love. But later, {name} jumps all over every guest, cries whenever you leave the room, and growls if someone approaches the sofa. It is hard to see at first, but many problem behaviors start right there.

## why
A Dog does not interpret affection the way people do. Being lifted off the ground means losing control of their own body: many dogs tolerate it, but very few truly enjoy it. Kissing their face means looming directly into their personal space—a gesture that feels confrontational and uncomfortable among dogs. And sleeping on your bed or sofa teaches that prime real estate belongs to them and that attention is on tap whenever demanded. Much of what is labeled "bad behavior" begins right here: treating dogs like human babies or plush toys rather than honoring them as dogs.

## steps
1. Keep your Dog on all four paws. If they beg to be picked up by putting paws on your legs, do not lift them: wait without eye contact until all four paws are on the floor.
2. Give them their own dog bed in a quiet corner of the home as their dedicated sleeping spot. Your bed and the sofa remain yours.
3. If they climb onto the sofa or bed, calmly walk into their space without touching or speaking until they step down. Then direct them to their bed.
4. Swap face kisses for gentle strokes on the chest or side when they are calm and voluntarily seek affection.
5. Greet them only when calm. If they jump upon your return, wait silently without looking; once all four paws touch the floor, greet them calmly.

## example
### {name} and the living room sofa
You arrive home and {name} greets you jumping and whimpering, begging to be picked up. This time, you keep your hands still. You wait in the entryway without eye contact until all four paws rest on the floor. Only then do you gently stroke their chest. That evening, {name} hops onto the sofa. You stand up and calmly walk into their space without speaking or touching until they step down and head to their bed. You speak a soft "good" Marker. During the first week they try repeatedly; gradually, their own bed wins out.

## compare
### do
Keep them on the floor, provide their own bed, and greet them only when calm with gentle chest strokes.
### dont
Pick them up when whimpering, let them sleep in your bed, and smother their face with kisses. Done with love, this only teaches constant demanding.

## tip
Try it today: spend an entire day without picking them up, allowing them in your bed, or kissing their face. Note how many times they demand it.

## takeaways
- Loving your Dog means treating them like a dog.
- Being carried, shared beds, and face kisses are human habits, not canine needs.
- Their bed is their spot; the sofa and your bed are yours.
- Behavioral issues often start from not treating a Dog like a dog.
