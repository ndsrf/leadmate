# Let Them Sniff

## heading
For your Dog, the walk is a long read of smells

## hook
You are on your way to the park and {name} stops at every lamp post and bush. You tug gently, say "come on", and keep going, because the walk has a destination. The leash tightens, {name} looks back at the smell, and neither of you enjoys the next block. It feels like a small fight over nothing, repeated all day.

## why
Smell is how a Dog reads the street: who passed, how long ago, what is worth knowing. Sniffing is also calming, because it lowers Arousal and keeps your Dog thinking instead of reacting. A walk with no sniffing builds frustration, and frustration shows up as pulling. The way out is not to ban smells but to make them part of the deal. When {name} looks at you of its own accord, that is a Check-in. Mark it, then give "Go Sniff": the smell becomes the reward, and looking at you becomes the way to earn it.

## steps
1. Walk with a Slack leash and watch for a Scent Spot ahead: a lamp post, a bush, a corner where {name} slows and the nose drops.
2. Wait for {name} to look at you, and mark that glance with a soft "good". Do not call or pull; the look has to be {name}'s idea.
3. Say "Go Sniff" and stand still, keeping the leash Slack in a U while {name} reads the spot for as long as it likes.
4. When {name} lifts the head, say "Let's Go" and walk on together. Repeat at the next smell, a few times per walk at first.

## example
### {name} at the lamp post outside the bakery
{name} slows down near the lamp post by the bakery, nose low. You stop and keep the leash hanging in a U. Nothing happens for a few seconds, then {name} glances up at you. You say "good", give a treat beside your leg and say "Go Sniff". {name} reads the post for about ten seconds while you wait. When the head comes up you say "Let's Go" and walk on. The next lamp post, the glance comes a little sooner.

## compare
### do
Wait for the look, mark it, then give "Go Sniff" and let your Dog finish reading while the leash hangs Slack.
### dont
Keep walking while your Dog strains toward the smell on a Taut leash; the pull wins the smell and teaches your Dog that pulling works.

## tip
Try it today: on your next walk, pick three smells you are happy to wait at. At each one, wait for a look, mark it and give "Go Sniff".

## takeaways
- Sniffing is your Dog's main job on a walk, not a delay.
- A Check-in before "Go Sniff" turns the smell into a reward.
- Give "Go Sniff" with a Slack leash and let your Dog finish.
- Pulling toward a smell should never be what gets it there.
