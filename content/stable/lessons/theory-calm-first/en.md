# Calm First

## heading
Barking and unable to listen

## hook
A guest arrives and {name} erupts into nonstop barking. You say "sit," and nothing happens. You repeat louder, still nothing. You wave a treat, and barking escalates further. It seems as though they cannot hear you. In a very real sense, they cannot: in that moment, listening is physiologically impossible.

## why
A highly aroused Dog cannot learn. When nonstop barking or frantic running takes over the house, their body enters high alert: heart rate spikes and the brain locks onto whatever triggered the reaction. In that state, your words are mere background noise. Asking for behaviors results in failure and teaches them to ignore you. Waving a treat reinforces the high arousal. And shouting injects even more chaotic energy. The first priority is downshifting: waiting for calm to return without adding stimulation. Only then can your Dog listen and learn.

## steps
1. When nonstop barking or frantic running starts, ask for nothing. No Cues, no treats, no shouting: any energy you inject only winds them up further.
2. Wait still, without speaking or making eye contact. Breathe slowly and keep arms relaxed at your sides: your calm helps them settle.
3. If frantic running erupts through the house, calmly walk into their path without touching to interrupt momentum, then wait again.
4. As soon as they sit or lie down and look at you, listening is possible. Now you can ask for an easy behavior with your Cue.
5. Reward calm: a soft "good" Marker and a treat once they lie down and relax their body.

## example
### {name} and the guest
The doorbell rings, your sister enters, and {name} starts barking and circling the living room. Previously, you would repeat "sit" over and over. Today you say nothing: you remain standing, motionless, with arms relaxed. {name} barks a little more, gradually decreasing. Finally, they sit down and check in with you. Now they are ready: you give your down Cue, and down they go. You speak a soft "good" Marker and deliver a treat. The first attempt takes nearly two minutes; a week later, under sixty seconds.

## compare
### do
Wait motionless and silent until calm arrives, and cue a behavior only once they sit or lie down and check in with you.
### dont
Repeat "sit" or "quiet" louder and louder while barking continues: they cannot hear you and learn to ignore your voice.

## tip
Try it today: next time nonstop barking begins, stay silent and count the seconds until calm returns. Note it down to compare later.

## takeaways
- A highly aroused Dog cannot learn.
- Calm first; Cue second.
- Add no energy: no demands, no shouting, no treats.
- Reward calm the moment it appears.
