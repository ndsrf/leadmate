# The Poste Technique

## heading
Pulling leads nowhere

## hook
{name} spots something exciting, lunges, and you feel the yank down your arm. Instinct urges you to follow for a few steps to avoid a struggle, or to yank backward to slam the brakes. Both feel reasonable in the moment, and both guarantee even harder pulling tomorrow.

## why
To your Dog, the leash conveys information. If pulling moves you forward, even by half a step, they learn that pulling brings them closer to what they want: pulling succeeded. If you yank backward, their body reflexively pushes against the pressure. It is opposition reflex—the exact same instinct that causes you to brace forward when someone pushes you from behind—and soon both of you are locked in a tug-of-war. Poste breaks that cycle: while the leash is Taut, nothing happens—no forward movement, no backward yank. When it goes Slack, the path reopens. Your Dog does not need a lecture: the rule is learned through repetition.

## steps
1. Keep your leash hand anchored near your waist, so you instantly detect when tension begins to build.
2. As soon as the leash goes Taut, stop completely: feet still, arm anchored to your core, without yanking backward.
3. Wait in silence. Do not call or repeat Cues: allow your Dog the space to discover the solution independently.
4. When the leash goes Slack—because they turn around, step back, or look at you—mark that moment with a gentle "good".
5. Resume walking immediately. Forward momentum is the reward; if you wish, add a treat right beside your leg.

## example
### {name} and the bakery scent
You walk past the bakery and {name} lunges toward the doorway. The leash goes Taut and you perform Poste, hand anchored near your hip. {name} strains, sniffs the air, and strains again. A few long seconds tick by. Suddenly they turn around to see why forward progress has stopped, and the leash drops Slack. "Good"—and you resume walking together. Ten meters ahead they try again, but turn back much faster this time. By the third attempt, they do not even tighten the leash.

## compare
### do
Stop with your arm anchored to your body and wait for the leash to go Slack. The moment it relaxes, resume walking.
### dont
Take "just one small step" while the leash is Taut to prevent a struggle. That single step reinforces the pull.

## tip
Try it today: on a quiet stretch of your street, perform Poste every time the leash goes Taut. Just five minutes, and note how quickly they offer Slack.

## takeaways
- If you move while they pull, pulling works.
- If you yank backward, they learn to pull harder.
- Slack leash, open path: move forward the instant tension drops.
