# Getting Ready for the Café

## heading
You want a coffee with {name}

## hook
It is Saturday and you want a coffee on the terrace with {name}. You sit at the first free table, right in the waiters' path. {name} gets up every time someone walks by, the leash runs across the aisle, and you never get to taste your coffee. Ten minutes later you both leave worn out. With a little preparation, the first visit can be much easier.

## why
A terrace packs many things together: food smells, people passing, plates clinking and chairs scraping. A Dog with energy still unspent finds it much harder to relax there. If it arrives after a long sniffing walk, it has already let some of that energy out. A corner by the wall has less movement around it, and a mat that smells of home tells the Dog where to rest.

## steps
1. Before you leave, take a long sniffing walk so {name} arrives with energy already spent and ready to rest.
2. Pick a table in a corner or by the wall, out of the waiters' path, and place {name} on the wall side.
3. Before sitting down, ask the venue whether dogs are welcome on the terrace: each place decides, and it is better to know before you go in.
4. Spread a mat that smells of home beside your chair, and bring something to chew and a few small treats.
5. Plan a first visit of about 10 minutes. If it gets hard, shorten it or move to a quieter table: leaving in time is also a success.

## example
### {name} in the café corner
On a Saturday morning you walk for ten minutes and {name} sniffs at their own pace. Before going in you ask at the counter whether you can sit outside, and they say yes. You choose the corner table by the wall, with {name} on the wall side. You spread the mat and hand over something to chew. When {name} lies down on it, you drop a treat. The waiters pass on the other side and {name} glances at them for a moment and goes back to chewing. After ten minutes you leave calmly.

## compare
### do
Choose a corner by the wall, with {name} on the wall side and the leash short, away from the waiters' path.
### dont
Sit in the waiters' path, with the leash running across the aisle and {name} on the side where people pass. Every tray becomes something new to watch.

## tip
Try it today: without leaving home, spread a mat and let {name} lie on it with something to chew. It will be their spot on the terrace.

## takeaways
- Arrive after a long sniffing walk, with the energy already spent.
- Pick a corner by the wall, out of the waiters' path.
- Bring a mat that smells of home and something to chew.
- Keep the first visit to about 10 minutes; ask the venue about dogs.
