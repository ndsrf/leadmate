# Eating Non-Food Objects

## heading
Swallowing socks, stones, and napkins

## hook
In an unguarded moment, {name} scoops up a sock and swallows it whole. Or crunches on rocks during walks, or raids napkins from the trash. Panic strikes, you try to pry it away, and they bolt. There are two distinct issues here: what can be trained, and what requires veterinary attention. We begin by separating them.

## why
Some dogs, especially puppies and adolescents, explore the world through their mouths and ingest whatever they find. Others act out of boredom, restlessness, or having learned that grabbing forbidden items guarantees Handler attention. Occasionally, an underlying medical condition (such as pica) is responsible, which only a veterinarian can diagnose. If you try to pry items away by force, your Dog learns to swallow faster or run away to hide. Training a reliable drop Cue and trading for higher-value items works far better than playing chase.

## steps
1. If something has already been swallowed or symptoms appear, see your vet immediately. Everything that follows is behavioral training, not emergency care.
2. Put commonly stolen items out of reach: laundry socks, plastic bags, trash cans behind secure lids, and garden stones. Prevention is safer than training.
3. If you see them eyeing an item, calmly walk to the object and pick it up yourself before they grab it. No rushing, no shouting.
4. If it is already in their mouth, do not chase: chasing turns it into a high-stakes game. Show a delicious treat and wait for them to approach.
5. When they release the item to take the treat, say "good", deliver the reward, and casually pick up the object. It is a trade: {name} always comes out ahead.
6. Practice the "drop it" Cue daily using toys: offer a higher-value treat in exchange for each item.

## example
### {name} and the sock
You are folding laundry and {name} trots into the living room with a sock. You do not chase: you grab a high-value treat and sit on the floor, looking away. {name} approaches curiously, drops the sock to sniff the treat, and eats it. Meanwhile, you calmly retrieve the sock. That evening, you move the laundry basket behind a closed door.

## compare
### do
Trade the object for a higher-value treat, without rushing, and dog-proof items commonly stolen.
### dont
Chase them down shouting to pry the object away: that turns it into a game and encourages swallowing faster.

## tip
Today: walk through your home at {name}'s eye level and stow away five items that could be swallowed. Note whether any have been stolen before.

## takeaways
- If swallowed, call the veterinarian immediately.
- Prevention is safer than training.
- Never chase: trade for something better.
- If compulsive ingestion persists, consult your vet.
