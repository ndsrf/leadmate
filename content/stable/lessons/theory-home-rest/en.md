# Home Is for Resting

## heading
Demanding attention all day long

## hook
You are sitting on the sofa and {name} drops a ball onto your lap. You toss it down the hallway. Two minutes later they return, nudge your hand with their snout, and stare. You talk, pet, and play a little. And so it goes all day long. By evening, when you want to unwind, {name} keeps demanding attention, and if ignored, begins to bark.

## why
Believing that granting free rein over the entire house is kinder is a myth: total freedom requires constant vigilance, multiplies risks, and complicates housetraining control. To settle down, your Dog needs a clearly defined safe space. The ideal size is just enough to stand up, turn 360 degrees, and stretch out fully on their side; more space is not better. Indoors is where dogs learn to rest, not patrol or demand endless attention.

## steps
1. Decide that indoor games like fetch and chasing are off the table. High-intensity play and exercise happen outside, at the park or on the street.
2. Set up a safe resting haven in a quiet zone away from busy hallways, with a defined bed, open crate, or pen, and an appropriate chew toy.
3. When they solicit attention outside times you initiate, do not look, speak, or touch. To a Dog, making eye contact is already a response.
4. If they persist, stand up and calmly walk into their space without speaking or touching (the Send away) until they move off to their resting haven.
5. When they retreat to their spot and lie down on their own, mark with a soft "good" Marker and leave them to rest. Never use this haven as punishment.

## example
### {name} and the resting corner
You are reading on the sofa and {name} drops a toy into your lap. You do not make eye contact or say a word. {name} nudges with a nose. You stand up and calmly walk into their space with arms down. {name} steps back toward their safe haven, finds their chew, and begins gnawing peacefully. You speak a soft "good" Marker and resume reading. Nobody disturbs them while resting.

## compare
### do
Place the safe haven in a quiet spot just large enough to stand, turn, and stretch out, using the calm Send away if attention is demanded.
### dont
Give free rein over the entire house expecting better rest, place the bed in busy hallways, or use the haven as a place of punishment.

## tip
Try it today: set up their safe haven in a quiet spot without foot traffic, ensure they can stretch out comfortably, and remove interactive toys from the living room.

## takeaways
- Home is for resting; play and intense exercise happen outdoors.
- Total household freedom causes hypervigilance; a defined haven brings peace.
- The space should allow standing, turning, and stretching out without being oversized.
- Position the haven in a quiet area away from busy traffic paths.
