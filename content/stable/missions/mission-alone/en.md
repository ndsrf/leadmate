# Learning to Stay Alone

## target
For several days, practice the step-by-step protocol with {name}: open door, distance, one-second micro-close, and release Cue. If there is panic or time runs out, open up and return to a shorter step.

## success
Tolerates the micro-close and the release Cue without distress, remaining calm in its space.

## problems
### cries-at-once
- label: Whines as soon as we separate
- reassure: It is very common: for {name}, separating from you is the hardest thing in the house.
- cause: The step is probably too large, or you are leaving right when arousal is highest.
- c1: Return to the previous step: door open and you in sight. Before leaving, a walk and a low-key goodbye.
- c2: If {name} drools, tries to escape, refuses treats, hurts itself, or damages the door when left alone, stop training, open the door, and consult your veterinarian or a canine behavior professional. This app does not replace professional care.
- easier: Step away just one step with the door open and return before any vocalization.

- caption: One step, then return

### panic
- label: Panics
- reassure: It is not misbehavior or stubbornness: it is fear, addressed with calm and patience.
- cause: If drooling, scratching at the door, trying to escape, or refusing treats occurs, the step was too large.
- c1: Open the door immediately without making a fuss, and drop to a much smaller step the next day. Do not wait for exhaustion.
- c2: If {name} drools, tries to escape, refuses treats, hurts itself, or damages the door when left alone, stop training, open the door, and consult your veterinarian or a canine behavior professional. This app does not replace professional care.
- easier: Pause training for a couple of days and consult a professional before continuing.
- alt: The Handler opens the pen door and the Dog steps out calmly
- caption: If there is panic, open up

### cries-at-close
- label: Whines when I close the door
- reassure: It is normal at first: door closure is the new step and {name} notices it.
- cause: The micro-close might still be too long, or the release Cue is not distinct.
- c1: Close the door for just one second, open it, and give the release Cue. Increase duration very gradually; with young Dogs under 4 months wait at most 2 minutes, and 10 with older Dogs.
- c2: If {name} drools, tries to escape, refuses treats, hurts itself, or damages the door when left alone, stop training, open the door, and consult your veterinarian or a canine behavior professional. This app does not replace professional care.
- easier: Close for half a second, open, and reward, three times in a row.
- alt: The Handler closes the pen door for one second and opens it
- caption: One second closed

## general
- reassure: Staying alone is difficult for many Dogs and the Handlers who care for them. Every small step counts.
- cause: Moving faster than {name} can handle is the most common pitfall.
- c1: When in doubt between two steps, choose the smaller one. Better an extra day than a setback.
- c2: If {name} drools, tries to escape, refuses treats, hurts itself, or damages the door when left alone, stop training, open the door, and consult your veterinarian or a canine behavior professional. This app does not replace professional care.
- easier: Repeat yesterday's step today without progressing.

- caption: Small steps
