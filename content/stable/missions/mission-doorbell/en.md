# Doorbell Drill

## target
Have someone ring the doorbell and reward your Dog once it settles on its mat.

## success
Goes to the mat or calms down on it in under one minute.

## problems
### barks
- label: Barks relentlessly
- reassure: Barking at the doorbell is extremely common. It has been reinforced over time.
- cause: The live doorbell is too intense to start: arousal surges before any Cue can be heard.
- c1: Use a recorded chime on your phone at low volume, increasing volume gradually.
- c2: Reward the very first pause in silence, even if it lasts one second.
- easier: Settle quietly on the mat with a low-volume recorded chime, 3 times.
- alt: A phone plays a quiet chime beside a calm Dog
- caption: Start with a quiet chime

### door
- label: Will not leave the door
- reassure: Rushing the door is natural: that is where the action happens.
- cause: The mat may be positioned either too close to the doorway or too far to remember.
- c1: Place the mat a few paces back from the door, where the Dog can observe without reaching.
- c2: Practice moving to the mat without any doorbell, rewarding heavily multiple times a day.
- easier: Send to the mat on Cue without a chime, 3 times.
- alt: A training mat positioned a few steps from the front door
- caption: Mat placed a few steps back

### mat
- label: Refuses to go to the mat
- reassure: The mat carries no reinforcement history yet. Value must be built.
- cause: The Dog has never experienced reinforcement for staying on the mat.
- c1: Scatter high-value treats on the mat when unobserved, making it a rewarding discovery zone.
- c2: Use the Marker every time the Dog steps onto the mat independently.
- easier: Step onto the mat independently, 3 times.
- alt: A Dog discovers high-value treats scattered across its mat
- caption: The mat as a rewarding zone

## general
- reassure: Visitors generate immense arousal. A challenging repetition does not undo prior learning.
- cause: Raising criteria too rapidly is the standard mistake.
- c1: Practice without real visitors present, working solely on the auditory cue.
- c2: Keep drills short, limited to two or three reps.
- easier: Settle on the mat with a gentle knock at the door, 2 times.

- caption: Short training sets
