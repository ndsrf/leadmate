# A Better Trade

## target
During three days, put away what {name} usually grabs, and if an object is taken, do not chase: trade the object for a higher-value treat.

## success
Releases the object in exchange for the treat without running away.

## problems
### swallowed
- label: Swallowed an object
- reassure: Accidents happen, even with vigilant supervision. Taking swift action is what matters now.
- cause: Ingested foreign bodies do not always pass safely, and internal severity cannot be judged from the outside.
- c1: Call your veterinarian promptly and report what was swallowed and when, without waiting for symptoms.
- c2: If {name} swallowed a non-food item, vomits, refuses food, shows abdominal pain, or repeatedly ingests foreign objects, call your veterinarian immediately. Do not wait for it to pass and do not induce vomiting at home.
- easier: Secure all ingestible objects today and resume training only once cleared by your veterinarian.
- alt: The Handler makes a phone call next to the Dog
- caption: Contact the veterinarian

### runs
- label: Runs off with the item
- reassure: Expected response: to {name}, running off with contraband turns into a game of chase.
- cause: Being pursued after grabbing items reinforces the flee-and-keep behavior.
- c1: Do not chase: sit down, present a high-value treat, and wait for the Dog to approach.
- c2: If {name} swallowed a non-food item, vomits, refuses food, shows abdominal pain, or repeatedly ingests foreign objects, call your veterinarian immediately. Do not wait for it to pass and do not induce vomiting at home.
- easier: Practice the trade game with a low-value toy, 2 times.
- alt: The Handler sits on the floor showing a treat as the Dog approaches
- caption: Do not chase

### again
- label: Repeatedly grabs items
- reassure: Resisting accessible temptation is hard; management prevents the opportunity.
- cause: Items likely remain accessible or the trade game is not yet solid.
- c1: Audit your space at Dog eye level and secure loose items; practice trades daily using toys.
- c2: If {name} swallowed a non-food item, vomits, refuses food, shows abdominal pain, or repeatedly ingests foreign objects, call your veterinarian immediately. Do not wait for it to pass and do not induce vomiting at home.
- easier: Secure only the most frequently grabbed objects for one day.
- alt: The Handler places small household items into a high storage box
- caption: Out of reach

## general
- reassure: Managing the environment while training is demanding. Every secured item eliminates an emergency vet risk.
- cause: Attempting training drills before managing environmental access is the classic error.
- c1: Secure the environment first and train second: prevention is the safest path.
- c2: If {name} swallowed a non-food item, vomits, refuses food, shows abdominal pain, or repeatedly ingests foreign objects, call your veterinarian immediately. Do not wait for it to pass and do not induce vomiting at home.
- easier: Secure five items the Dog frequently targets.
- alt: A tidy living room with small items stored safely on a high shelf
- caption: Secure first
