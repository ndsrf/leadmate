# Leave It on the Sidewalk

## target
When spotting discarded food or trash on the ground, give the Cue "Leave It" once and reward when the Dog looks back at you.

## success
Passes by the food without picking it up.

## problems
### grabs
- label: Grabs it before I can speak
- reassure: Dogs are lightning-fast scavengers. It is pure instinct.
- cause: You noticed the ground food too late.
- c1: Scan the ground several meters ahead on your path.
- c2: Use Leash Gathering when passing known litter zones.
- easier: Spot the food before your Dog does and deliver the "Leave It" Cue, 2 times.
- alt: A Handler scans the ground ahead of the Dog
- caption: Scan ahead

### ignores
- label: Ignores me
- reassure: Struggling on the sidewalk is normal: outdoor environments carry high distraction.
- cause: The Cue has not been proofed strongly enough outside the home.
- c1: Revisit indoor practice using low-value food items.
- c2: Offer a reward higher in value than the ground item.
- easier: Practice "Leave It" at home with a treat on the floor, 3 times.
- alt: Practicing "Leave It" at home with a treat on the floor
- caption: Return to indoor practice

### repeat
- label: Had to repeat the Cue multiple times
- reassure: Repeating a Cue is a natural human reflex. What matters is the effort to practice.
- cause: Repetition dilutes Cue salience, turning it into background noise.
- c1: Deliver the Cue once and wait.
- c2: If there is no response, calmly guide away from the food without speaking.
- easier: Deliver "Leave It" once and step away, 2 times.
- alt: A Handler moves away from ground food with the Dog in silence
- caption: Cue once

## general
- reassure: Ground food is among the most demanding real-world Distractors. You are making progress.
- cause: Introducing the skill in high-distraction street environments first is the common error.
- c1: Keep practice sets short at home.
- c2: Increase environmental difficulty gradually.
- easier: Practice "Leave It" indoors with a toy on the floor, 2 times.
