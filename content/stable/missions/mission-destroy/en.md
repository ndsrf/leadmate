# Chews at Hand

## target
During three days, leave items {name} usually chews out of reach, distribute two chews around the home, and trade for one whenever you catch chewing on forbidden objects.

## success
No new destruction occurs and {name} chooses its chews.

## problems
### ignores
- label: Ignores the chew toy
- reassure: Many Dogs are indifferent to the first chew texture offered.
- cause: That particular chew is not appealing, or something more tempting remains accessible.
- c1: Try a different texture or stuff a toy with food; scent and flavor boost interest.
- c2: Put away prized household targets so the chew is the only available option.
- easier: Offer a food-stuffed chew once a day.
- alt: A food-stuffed toy rests on the floor as the Dog chews on it
- caption: A flavored chew

### again
- label: Destroyed something anyway
- reassure: An accident does not erase past success. Everyone misses an item occasionally.
- cause: An item was left within reach or under-stimulation set in.
- c1: Do a sweep at Dog eye level and secure loose items; scolding after the fact achieves nothing.
- c2: Add a decompression sniff walk before leaving the Dog unsupervised.
- easier: One full day with zero target items within reach.
- alt: The Handler crouches down at Dog height to inspect the living room
- caption: Inspect at Dog height

### alone
- label: Destroys things only when left alone
- reassure: Happening exclusively in your absence is key diagnostic data, not a failure.
- cause: It could stem from boredom or isolation distress; if barking or howling accompanies it, separation anxiety may be involved.
- c1: Provide an accessible stuffed chew before departing, and burn off energy with a sniff walk.
- c2: If barking, howling, or distress occurs upon departure, consult a canine behavior professional.
- easier: Leave a food-stuffed chew when stepping out, once.
- alt: The Dog lies by the door enjoying a stuffed chew toy
- caption: Exercised and engaged

## general
- reassure: Dog-proofing a home takes ongoing effort. Every secured object prevents a destructive event.
- cause: Trying to audit every room in a single day is overwhelming.
- c1: Start with one room and focus on the most targeted household items.
- c2: Review the "Destructive Chewing" lesson.
- easier: Secure favorite chew targets in a single room.
- alt: A tidy room featuring a chew toy on the floor
- caption: Start room by room
