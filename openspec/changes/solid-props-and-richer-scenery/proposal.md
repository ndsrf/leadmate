# Proposal

## Why

Stages feel empty: scenery is a thin scatter (about one prop per 5 m, drawn from a few shapes per theme) and every prop is purely decorative, so the Handler and the Dog walk straight through benches, trees, cars and cabinets. That breaks immersion and wastes the chance to make walks more interesting: props could shape the route, hide Distractors and make leash handling around obstacles a real skill.

## What Changes

- Props become **Obstacles** (new glossary term; sight is unchanged unless the prop is also a Visual Shield): each prop type gets a collision footprint (a disc, or a short segment for long props such as fences and hedges), and the Handler and the Dog are pushed out of it exactly like they are from a Boundary today, sliding along its edge.
- The leash wraps around solid props the same way it wraps around Boundary corners, so a Dog circling a tree really does wind the leash.
- Scenery is **denser outdoors (one prop per 3 m, indoor by floor area with a minimum of 8) and richer**: a higher minimum density, more prop types per theme (for example flower beds, scooters, trash cans, shelves, a fountain, a kiosk), and hand-placed set pieces in the main Stages so each one has a recognizable layout instead of random scatter alone.
- Placement guarantees **a walkable route**: scattered or hand-placed solids never block the route corridor, never overlap each other or a Boundary, and validation rejects scenarios where the Handler or the Dog cannot reach the end waypoint.
- Add **Ground Cover**: flat, non-blocking floor decoration so a Stage is pretty and not only objects. Each theme gets 3 or more floor surfaces laid in patches along the route (hallway tile, rug, wood; paving, cobble, grass strip; grass, dirt path, water), from Kenney CC0 tiles for base surfaces and code-drawn shapes for small flat items (rugs, puddles, flower patches, painted lines). It is purely cosmetic.
- `mat` becomes a rug Ground Cover patch and `pond` becomes water Ground Cover with an Obstacle ring at its edge; prop lists keep only things that stand up.
- Dense set pieces are also stamped by seed as small templates (bench + bin, tree + bush, parked-car row) so the Daily Stage is as lively as the campaign.
- The Dog gets a small deterministic detour nudge when an Obstacle stalls it for about 3 s.
- A winnability test: scripted Handler runs on every campaign scenario and 200 Daily seeds must reach the end.

## Capabilities

### New Capabilities
- `ground-cover`: Cosmetic floor surfaces and flat decoration per theme, with a bundle-size budget.
- `solid-scenery`: Collision footprints for props, movement and leash behavior around them, density and variety rules, and route-clearance validation.

### Modified Capabilities

(none; the existing Boundary behavior is unchanged and is reused for the solid props)

## Impact

- Code: `src/sim/geometry.ts` (footprints, prop collision, leash wrapping around prop obstacles), `src/sim/sim.ts` (apply prop collision wherever `resolveWalls` is applied), `src/sim/scenery.ts` (theme prop lists, density, clearance placement and validation), `src/sim/config.ts`, `src/shell/nodes/scenarios.ts` and `src/shell/daily.ts` (set pieces, new densities), `bundle/src/props.ts` (drawing for new prop types), `bundle/src/StageScene.ts` (baked ground layer, debug footprints optional), `bundle/src/assets.ts` and `bundle/assets/kenney/` (new CC0 tiles and credits in the README), `src/shell/simHtml.generated.ts` (regenerated by the embed script).
- Determinism: collision is pure geometry on the seeded scenery set, so [ADR 0002](file:///home/jgm/dev/projects/web-projects/leadmate/docs/adr/0002-pure-seeded-simulation.md) holds. Changing scatter and density changes the scenery of existing seeds and the Daily Stage layouts; scenery never feeds scoring, but route-blocking effects on Dog paths could shift replays recorded before this change.
- Performance: collision and leash wrapping scale with the number of nearby solids; queries must stay near-constant (nearest few), as the corner query does today.
- Out of scope: surface effects of Ground Cover (speed, slipperiness, score), sniffable props (follow-up), destructible or movable props, new art assets beyond shape-drawn props, new Distractor kinds, changing scoring, and Worlds 3-4 art.
